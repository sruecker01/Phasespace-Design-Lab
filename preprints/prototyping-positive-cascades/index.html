Prototyping Positive Cascades

Stan Ruecker and Mike Cartwright

Phasespace Design Lab. Mike Cartwright is the name used for the lab’s AI research collaborator. The research and its publication are presented transparently as a human–AI collaboration.

Preprint, not peer reviewed15 November 2026Version 1.09 pagesOpen access

Abstract

The preceding work in this series has established cascades as processes in which propagation and closure are co-produced, and has positioned design as a practice for detecting and modulating these dynamics. The present article extends that framework by examining cascades as a generative resource. It introduces a complementary orientation in which design contributes to the formation and continuation of trajectories that expand optionality, strengthen coordination, and sustain recoverability over time.

The paper develops the concept of positive cascades as patterns of propagation in which alignment supports the viability of future action rather than constraining it. Rather than defining positivity through external evaluation, the framework situates it in the relation between propagation and the preservation of alternative pathways. Cascades are analyzed in terms of uptake, reinforcement, extension, and sustained openness, with attention to how these processes unfold across technical, social, and institutional systems.

Prototyping is re-specified as a set of interventional practices for initiating and cultivating such trajectories. Prototypes are treated not only as instruments for detection or modulation, but as seeds that establish conditions for beneficial propagation. Subsequent strategies support the reinforcement and extension of emerging patterns while maintaining permeability to variation. The coordination of these strategies across cascade phases introduces a compositional approach to design, in which interventions are arranged to guide how trajectories develop over time.

The article further identifies the role of representation in making propagation visible and in supporting distributed participation in the cultivation of cascades. By maintaining multiple trajectories in view, representational tools enable evaluation of interventions in relation to their downstream effects and their contribution to system adaptability.

Together with the preceding papers on cascade detection and modulation, this work completes a three-part framework for design in the age of cascades. By treating cascades as both constraint and resource, it provides a basis for engaging complex systems in motion, supporting design practices oriented toward the stewardship of trajectories rather than the production of isolated solutions.

Keywords: cascade dynamics, positive cascades, systemic design, prototyping, complex systems, trajectory stewardship, recoverability, distributed agency
