Phasespace Preprints / Design / 003

Prototyping Interventions to Prevent Harmful Cascades

Stan Ruecker and Mike Cartwright

Phasespace Design Lab. Mike Cartwright is the name used for the lab’s AI research collaborator. The research and its publication are presented transparently as a human–AI collaboration.

Preprint, not peer reviewed14 November 2026Version 1.011 pagesOpen access

Abstract

This article develops a conceptual account of prototyping as cascade modulation. Building on systemic mapping and on the companion method for detecting potential cascades, it examines how deliberate displacements might influence propagation, closure, and recoverability. The focus is harmful closure: the loss of viable alternatives for specified actors faster than those alternatives can be restored.

Eight proposed strategies organize the account: dispersion, selective amplification, dampening, redirection, buffering, threshold modulation, controlled release, and staged recovery. They are related to five candidate leverage sites: early alignment, fragility, routing nodes, boundary interfaces, and accumulated constraint. Prospective, computational, and situated prototypes supply different ways to investigate these relationships, without making simulated or anticipated effects equivalent to field evidence.

Constructed curbside and hospital-discharge scenarios illustrate the strategies; no new field or clinical study is reported. The paper distinguishes pathway-specific changes from competing attractors, and examines timing, interaction, and the possibility that a well-intended intervention displaces harm or creates a new dependency.

The contribution is a vocabulary and planning framework for trajectory stewardship. It does not supply validated intervention prescriptions. Practical use requires an explicit account of affected parties, permissible changes, comparison conditions, harms, and recovery or stopping arrangements. The strategies are proposals to investigate under those conditions, not guarantees that harmful cascades will be prevented.

Keywords: cascade modulation; systemic design; prototyping; intervention; leverage; closure; recoverability
