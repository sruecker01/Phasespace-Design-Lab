Phasespace Preprints / Design / 002

Prototyping for Awareness of Potential Cascades

Stan Ruecker and Mike Cartwright

Phasespace Design Lab. Mike Cartwright is the name used for the lab’s AI research collaborator. The research and its publication are presented transparently as a human–AI collaboration.

Preprint, not peer reviewed14 November 2026Version 1.012 pagesOpen access

Abstract

This article introduces a cascade-aware extension to systemic prototyping, building on a model that replaces divergence–convergence cycles with displacement and the accumulation of overlapping systemic maps. Within this framework, prototypes are redefined as instruments for introducing controlled displacements that reveal how effects propagate through complex systems.

Cascades are specified as processes in which the space of possible trajectories contracts as transformations align, reducing optionality faster than it can be restored. This emphasis on closure complements existing accounts of propagation by foregrounding the conditions under which alternatives are preserved or eliminated. Design is repositioned as a practice that engages with these dynamics, influencing how cascades form, travel, and stabilize.

The methodology integrates three coordinated modalities of prototyping: prospective, computational, and situated. Each modality introduces displacements at a different level of engagement with the system and produces systemic maps that capture propagation pathways under specific conditions. These maps are retained and overlaid, allowing multiple warranted readings of the system to remain active while supporting a graded notion of evidentiary strength.

The approach is demonstrated through extended examples in urban curbside allocation, municipal pilot programs, and hospital discharge processes. Across these domains, the method reveals patterns of amplification, attenuation, and redirection, supporting evaluation of interventions in relation to their downstream effects.

The contribution lies in articulating an apparatus for trajectory stewardship, enabling designers to engage with systems in terms of the cascades they invite, suppress, or redirect. By making propagation, closure, and recoverability visible, the framework supports more deliberate interaction with systems characterized by ongoing transformation.

Keywords: prototyping, cascades, systemic mapping, cascade detection, trajectory stewardship, design research
