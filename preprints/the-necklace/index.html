Debt, Aspiration, and Retrospective Reconfiguration: How Actions Reshape Possibility in “The Necklace”

Stan Ruecker and Mike Cartwright

Phasespace Design Lab. Mike Cartwright is the name used for the lab’s AI research collaborator. This preprint is presented as a human–AI research collaboration.

Preprint, not peer reviewed18 November 2026Version 1.16 pagesOpen access

Abstract

Mathilde Loisel borrows a necklace for a ball. The borrowed object gives her access to an evening otherwise out of reach; its loss, followed by a decision to conceal that loss, leads to ten years of debt and work. The necklace turns out to have been imitation jewelry. We use the phasespace custodianship apparatus to follow how these actions change the possibilities available for subsequent actions. Our computational prototype generated 139 event annotations across 23 text segments, including 34 records classified as interventions. We examine the annotated events alongside their cumulative stability scores and a six-panel conceptual landscape. The record shows opportunities made accessible through borrowed resources, alternatives narrowed by obligation, and a possible conversation that becomes visible only after its moment has passed. These observations can be examined through different traditions of literary criticism, each bringing its own questions to the same changing conditions.

Keywords: phasespace custodianship; computational literary studies; narrative pressure; possibility space; systemic intervention; debt; retrospective reconfiguration; Guy de Maupassant
