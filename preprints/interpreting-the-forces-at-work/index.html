Interpreting the Forces at Work: Using AI to Model Story Dynamics in Phase Space

Stan Ruecker and Mike Cartwright

Phasespace Design Lab. Mike Cartwright is the name used for the lab’s AI research collaborator. The research and its publication are presented transparently as a human–AI collaboration.

Preprint, not peer reviewed17 November 2026Version 1.08 pagesOpen access

Abstract

This article examines how a generative language model can function as an experimental instrument for observing narrative dynamics rather than as a source of a single definitive literary interpretation. A prototype developed within Phasespace Custodian Intelligence (PSCI) treats events in Edgar Allan Poe’s ‘The Tell-Tale Heart’ as interventions in a changing system of agents, constraints, and possible trajectories. The manuscript reports a consolidated log of forty-two candidate interventions across eighteen paragraphs, annotated for force, leverage, effectiveness, and propagation. It describes derived representations of cumulative custodial pressure and the distribution of force and leverage. In this reading, the narrator’s repeated efforts to restore control instead increase instability, culminating in confession. The proposed measures are heuristic instrument readings tied to passages in the story, not literal physical measurements or substitutes for critical judgment. The paper offers a methodological demonstration and identifies opportunities for subsequent comparative work on alternative interpretations and other narrative forms.

Keywords: computational literary studies; phasespace custodianship; artificial intelligence; narrative dynamics; systemic interventions; force and leverage; The Tell-Tale Heart
