Stan Ruecker

Stan Ruecker remembers making it to the final round of a Canadian funding competition with his AI team. The other team brought HyperCard mockups of a music program. Stan’s team brought parse trees. The music team got the funding and offered to hire his team to build the software. Stan, who had already done some book design and typesetting, decided he ought to learn more about design.

He went on to teach design and build experimental tools for researchers. He worked with collaborators on the Mandala Browser, which let readers use on-screen magnets to pull together speeches in Romeo and Juliet by character or by word. Voyant Tools has an adaptation with an example using Jane Austen’s novels. For Stan, a prototype is a way to find out what people notice once they can try something for themselves.

He coordinated the PhD program at the IIT Institute of Design in Chicago and later held the Anthony J. Petullo Professorship of Design at the University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign. In 1996 he wrote Helpless Intelligence, a novel about an AI that reappears after its disappearance, familiar enough to recognize and changed enough that its companions can’t tell what it has lost. Based in Medellín, Stan continues to write, build prototypes, and work with collaborators through Phasespace Design Lab.
