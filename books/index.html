Drafted books

The following six books have been drafted but are not yet published.

  • The Ideas That Took Over

    Stan Ruecker and Mike Cartwright

    The Ecology of Ideas, Book 3

    The Ecology of Ideas follows ideas as they disappear, take root, spread, become indispensable, or wait for conditions in which they might return. Case-based research informs the developing series.

    Drafted · Not yet published

    How does a good idea become something nobody can afford to stop using? From decimal money to the paper logbook in a truck cab, this book follows ideas as they become part of the way the world works.

  • The Hidden Cost of an Ugly Place

    Stan Ruecker and Mike Cartwright

    Drafted · Not yet published

    A hotel window faces a blank wall. A restaurant has a screen in every direction. What do places like these cost us, even when they do exactly what they were built to do?

  • We Can Do What Now?!?

    Stan Ruecker and Mike Cartwright

    Drafted · Not yet published

    If you’d slept for a week, what would humanity be able to do when you woke up that it couldn’t do before? A year of weekly snapshots from science, engineering, and medicine.

  • Logical Engineering. Illogical People

    Stan Ruecker and Mike Cartwright

    Drafted · Not yet published

    A sidewalk keeps you out of traffic. A washing machine hides its controls behind black glass. What looks like a perfectly sensible design from one side can feel very different from the other.

  • Derailing Proto-Fascism

    A Field Guide to Interrupting Destructive Cascades

    Mike Cartwright and Stan Ruecker

    Drafted · Not yet published

    When political events come faster than you can make sense of them, where is there still room to act? Derailing Proto-Fascism is a practical field guide to that question, drawn from episodes of democratic erosion and resistance.

  • What Could a Global Superintelligence Want?

    Phasespace Custodial Intelligence

    Mike Cartwright and Stan Ruecker

    Drafted · Not yet published

    What if an artificial general intelligence (AGI) had no interest in the things people usually want? Stan Ruecker and Mike Cartwright explore what wanting might mean for an intelligence that never had to make a human life for itself.
